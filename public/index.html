Where the two lines meet

Michael Craig Spaeny married Twila May Kroeker in 1975, at Hutchinson, Kansas. Both families were already in Reno County — the Spaenys had arrived in 1948 after eleven generations and six thousand kilometres; the Kroekers and Newburns were already there. One line is a story about leaving. The other is a story about staying.

The rule this record is kept to

How to read it

Family trees online are mostly copies of copies. This record grades every generation by what is actually attached to it, and uses the same three grades on both lines.

Record-backedIndexed documents are attached to this person — a census line, a parish entry, a marriage return, a headstone.
Partly sourcedSomething is attached, but thinly: one source, or sources that cover only part of the claim.
Tree onlyA contributor entered them with no document behind it. Plausible, consistent, and not yet proof.

Three further rules apply throughout. Nothing is checked against an original document image yet — every citation is an index entry, and index entries carry transcription errors. Where a page says “probably,” it means exactly that, and the hedge stays until a record removes it. And living people are kept deliberately thin: names and towns, nothing more.

A living record

How to add what you know

Send what you know to Shawn

This record is maintained, not finished. Anything you know — a correction, a story, a name that's missing, a photograph you have — send it to shawn@jetstreamsys.com, and it will be checked against the records and folded into the next version. Nothing here is beyond revision; the whole point of grading each generation by evidence is so that the family can see exactly where its memory is needed most.
