## The surname question — a lead, NOT a finding

**Kroeker is, in origin, a Prussian Mennonite name.** GAMEO (the Global Anabaptist Mennonite
Encyclopedia Online) records it first at **Tiegenhagen in 1627**, in the Vistula delta
congregations — Danzig, Ladekopp, Rosenort, Fürstenwerder, Heubuden, Elbing, Königsberg —
spreading from there to Poland, Russia, and North and South America. Etymology is contested:
Low German *kröke*, "a bend in the road", or Middle Low German *Kröker*, a tavern keeper.

And the geography fits temptingly well. In 1874 roughly 1,900 Russian Mennonites settled some
60,000 acres across **Marion, McPherson, Harvey and Reno counties**, bringing Turkey Red wheat.
The first wave found Reno County's crops destroyed by grasshoppers and moved east to Harvey
County; **some returned to Reno County about ten years later** and farmed the southern and
northeastern parts of it. **Buhler, in Reno County, was founded in the heart of that
settlement.** The Volhynian colony of Karolswalde emigrated almost entirely to Kansas in
1874–75.

**Do not put this on the page as fact.** Three things cut against it:

1. The family was **Baptist** — New Hope Baptist Church — not Mennonite, at least by Opal's
   generation.
2. The given names are wrong for it. **Bueford Lewis** and **Charles Alford** are American
   names, not Low German Mennonite ones (Cornelius, Abraham, Peter, Johann, Heinrich).
3. **Seaman** is not a Mennonite surname either.

Any of those is explicable — families left the Mennonite church constantly, and a single
generation of out-marriage changes a naming pattern completely — but explicable is not
evidence. **The honest position is that the surname has a documented Mennonite origin and this
particular family's connection to it is unproven.** That is exactly the kind of claim the
paternal line refuses to make about Donald's medals, and the same rule applies here.

## Research plan, ordered by what will actually crack

**Track 1 — get Bueford documented (highest payoff, lowest effort)**

Everything above Gen 2 currently hangs on one user-submitted tree. Fix that first.

1. **Bueford Kroeker's own obituary**, May 1993 — *Hutchinson News*. It will name his parents,
   his birthplace, his siblings, his church and his burial plot in one document, and convert
   Gen 1 from "tree only" to sourced. The Reno County Genealogical Society indexes local
   obituaries; Kansas Historical Society holds the microfilm. **This is the single highest-value
   document to find.**
2. **His Find a Grave memorial**, presumably Fairlawn Burial Park — not yet located. Opal's
   obituary gives Fairlawn as *her* burial place; that Bueford is beside her is likely but
   **not stated**. Confirm with the cemetery office.
3. **Reno County marriage record, 7 June 1942** — Kansas licences name both sets of parents,
   which would independently confirm Charles Kroeker and Mary Emma Seaman.
4. **1920, 1930 and 1940 federal censuses, Reno County** — find Bueford as a child in Charles's
   household. The 1950 census is open and indexed and should show Bueford and Opal with their
   first children.
5. **Bueford's WWII draft registration.** Born 1916, he was of age; the Fourth Registration
   ("old man's registration") and the main 1940–47 series both survive and give birth date,
   birthplace, employer and next of kin.

**Track 2 — the Kroeker origin question**

Only worth working *after* Track 1 gives a documented Charles Kroeker with parents.

1. **Push Charles Alford Kroeker (1891–1948) back one generation.** His parents' names decide
   the Mennonite question outright. If they are Cornelius, Abraham, Johann or similar, the
   Mennonite descent becomes probable; if not, drop the hypothesis.
2. **Check the Mennonite genealogical databases directly** — GRANDMA (Genealogical Registry and
   Database of Mennonite Ancestry) is the standard, and Kroeker is heavily represented in it. A
   hit or a clean miss on a Kansas Charles Kroeker b. 1891 is decisive either way.
3. **Mennonite Library and Archives, Bethel College, North Newton, Kansas** — the repository for
   the 1874 Kansas migration, half an hour from Hutchinson.
4. **If the trail is not Mennonite**, the alternative is a straightforward German-American line;
   look for the immigrant generation in the 1880–1900 censuses and follow naturalisation records.

**Track 3 — the Newburn and Clune side**

1. **Leroy Newburn** — no dates, no grave, nothing. Start with **Viola Mae's 1980 obituary**
   (*Hutchinson News*, March 1980), which will name him and probably give his status at that
   date, then find his own.
2. **Viola Mae Clune, b. 30 May 1903** — find her parents. Clune is an Irish name and a rare one
   in Kansas, which makes it tractable.
3. **Resolve "Ruby Parks."** Opal's obituary names a sister Ruby Parks; Find a Grave has
   **Viola Ruth Newburn Parks (1931–2018), memorial 218844086**, daughter of Leroy and Viola
   (Clune) Newburn. Same parents, same surname, married name Parks — but "Ruby" against "Viola
   Ruth". Probably the same woman under a family nickname; possibly two sisters. Someone in the
   family will know at once.

**Track 4 — record the living, while they are here**

The paternal line lost Donald's war record to a fire in St. Louis. This line has the opposite
problem and the opposite opportunity: **eight of Bueford and Opal's ten children were alive in
2018, most of them in Hutchinson.** They hold the whole of this line's memory.

- Ask Twila directly: her grandparents' names, where the family farmed at Castleton, what was
  said about where the Kroekers came from, whether "Mennonite" was ever a word used at home.
- Ask about Lila and Kenny, the two children who died before 2022 — neither has dates here.
- Ask how Michael and Twila met, and what year each family arrived in Hutchinson.
- **Photographs.** Bueford died in 1993 and Opal in 2018; photographs of them certainly exist in
  the family, which is more than can be said for anyone on the paternal line before 1925.

- Parents: **Abraham Kroeker and Johanna Rosina Werrbach** (also written "Rosena Kroecker née

Household recorded at **Hutchinson city, Ward 5, in the 1900 and 1910 censuses** (Geni citing

So the family was in **Butler County by January 1880** and in **Hutchinson by November 1884**.

### What that does to the Mennonite question — it cuts both ways, and net against

**For:** Abraham. My own written test was "Cornelius, Abraham, Johann or Heinrich makes the
Mennonite descent probable." The name is there.

**Against, and heavier:**
1. **Butler County in 1880.** The Russian Mennonite settlement was in Marion, McPherson, Harvey
   and (from ~1884) Reno counties. Butler County — El Dorado, Augusta — had German settlers but
   no Mennonite colony. A Mennonite family arriving 1874–78 would not be in Butler County in 1880.
2. **Hutchinson city, not the Buhler/Inman farmland,** in 1900 and 1910.
3. **The children's names** — Henry, Rosa, Lizzie, John, Mary, Charles — are German-American
   Protestant, not Low German Mennonite. Only the father's name fits the pattern.
4. **"Johanna Rosina" is a Württemberg/Saxon double name, and Werbach is a town in the
   Main-Tauber district of Baden-Württemberg.** If Werrbach is a toponymic surname, Johanna's
   people were south-west German Lutherans or Catholics — which would explain the naming, the
   Butler County address, and the Baptist church two generations on.
5. The Baptist affiliation (New Hope) and the American given names of the next two generations.

**Revised position:** the surname's Mennonite origin is documented; this family's connection to
the 1874 Kansas migration is now **less likely than it looked this morning**, not more. Abraham
remains a genuinely Mennonite-pattern name and the question is still open — but the working
hypothesis should now be a **German (possibly Prussian-born) Kroeker who married a south-west
German woman and settled among ordinary German-Americans, not among the Mennonites.** The 1880
federal census for Butler County would settle it: it records birthplace for Abraham, Johanna,
and each of their parents.

**Candidate kin, unproven and now weakened:** the **Klaas Kroeker (1860–1942) family** — Peter
Kroeker's household from **Fuerstenwerder, Molotschna**, which emigrated **1878** to Reno County
near Buhler; Klaas was elder of **Bethel Mennonite Church, Inman** for thirty years and had a son
named Abraham. Geography and date once made this tempting. Butler County 1880 makes it unlikely
to be the same family.
