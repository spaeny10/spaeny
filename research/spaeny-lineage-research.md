## Three corrections to the family story

1. **Not Ellis Island.** Karl Anton married in St. Louis 2 May 1882; first child born 1884. Ellis Island opened January 1892. Arrival c. 1878–1882 → **Castle Garden** (1855–1890). The "immigrated to Ellis Island 1909" event on FamilySearch is attached to *both* Karl Anton and Sophia — a misfiled record, or a mid-life round trip to Switzerland.
2. **The name drifted, it wasn't changed at a desk.** Documented chain: **Späni** (Swiss parish registers, to 1861) → **Spaeni** ("Charles Spaeni", Missouri Marriages 1750–1920, 1882) → **Spane** ("Ernst Spane", NYC Births 1846–1909, 1893) → **Spaney** ("Chas Spaney", Kansas State Census 1905; "Ernest C Spaney", 1925) → **Spaeny** (headstone, 1939). Search all five, plus Spani, Spahni, Spöni.
3. **Heimatort is Canton Schwyz, not Thurgau.** Familiennamenbuch der Schweiz (3rd ed., 1989) lists Späni citizenship in SZ: Arth, Innerthal, Schübelbach, Steinen, Unteriberg (all pre-1800); Einsiedeln 1747; Illgau and Altendorf 19th c.; Lauerz 1945. Outliers: Mühlau AG, Hochdorf LU (both pre-1800). **Späni is not a Thurgau name at all** — Swiss records for Karl Anton will be in Schwyz archives.
   - Ignore FamilySearch's surname etymology for Späni (it gives Sardinian Italian *spanu*/Spanos — that's the unrelated Spanu/Spano).

## Donald Kenneth Spaeny — WWII

**Located:** Electronic Army Serial Number Merged File (RG 64, NARA AAD), indexed as **SPAENY, KENNETH D** (forename/middle transposed).
- Serial **37732174** · inducted **29 January 1944**, **Fort Leavenworth, Kansas**

**Not documented anywhere public:** unit, theatre, campaigns, decorations. Family memory says Europe; the timeline fits (inducted at 18, trained through mid-1944, when the ETO was absorbing infantry replacements) but there is **no evidence** placing him in Europe. Do not assert a division or medal list on inference.

**Why the file is gone:** the 12 July 1973 NPRC fire destroyed ~80% of Army personnel records for discharges 1 Nov 1912 – 1 Jan 1960. Donald's 1945/46 discharge is inside that window.

**Recovery routes, best first:**
1. **WD AGO Form 53-55** (honourable discharge certificate) — box 32 = battles/campaigns, box 33 = decorations. Ask Michael (Hutchinson) first, then **Mark's household in Springfield MO**, which holds nine of Donald's grandchildren and is the likeliest resting place for his papers. Also trace where Alberta's effects went in 2013.
2. **Rice County Register of Deeds**, 101 W Commercial St, Lyons, KS 67554 — Kansas counties recorded veterans' discharges; released to next of kin. Also try Barton Co. (Hoisington) and Reno Co. (Hutchinson).
3. **Kansas National Guard Military Archives**, 2722 SW Topeka Blvd, Topeka KS 66611, 785-646-0134 — holds WWII Statement of Service documents 1941–1946; SF-180, next of kin.
4. **NARA reconstruction request** — SF-180 + **NA Form 13055**; supply serial 37732174 and the induction date/place. Auxiliary sources: VA claims files, Multiple Name Pay Vouchers, Selective Service registration, GAO pay records, station hospital admissions.
5. **VA claim file** — separate storage, untouched by the fire.
6. Once a unit is known: morning reports and after-action reports at NARA College Park → campaign credit → service stars.
7. **American Legion Lysle Rishel Post 68** (he was Past Commander) and **Hoisington V.F.W. Post 7428** — life member of both; membership applications record unit and dates. Plus his *Hutchinson News* obituary, Dec 2011.

## Open research plan

**Track 1 — date the crossing (highest payoff)**
1. 1900 federal census, Queens Village, Queens NY — "year of immigration" and naturalisation columns. Repeat 1910/1920/1930.
2. NY passenger lists 1877–1882 (Castle Garden). Free at castlegarden.org; FamilySearch "New York Passenger Lists, 1820–1891". Search a Swiss male aged 16–21, all spellings, then by age/nationality/year with no surname.
3. Naturalisation: St. Louis city/county courts c. 1882–1895, then Rice Co. KS district court. Declaration of Intention names ship, port, arrival date. Missouri State Archives; Kansas Historical Society; NARA Kansas City.
4. Pull the 1909 manifest attached to both spouses — a returning-resident line states year of first arrival.
5. Ernest's 1917–18 WWI draft card + the indexed 1919 military registration (would settle the 30 vs 31 Oct 1893 birth-date discrepancy).

**Track 2 — prove the Swiss generations**
1. FamilySearch "Schweiz, Katholische und Lutherische Kirchenbücher, 1418–1996"; Illgau falls under **Muotathal parish**. Target gens 1–4, which have zero documents.
2. Staatsarchiv des Kantons Schwyz + Gemeinde Illgau and Steinen — **Bürgerregister** (communal citizenship registers track families regardless of residence).
3. Staatsarchiv Thurgau, Frauenfeld — Altnau, Weinfelden, Zihlschlacht registers; why Josef Anton left Schwyz (Thurgau textile/orchard labour demand); identify Anna Maria Vogelsang's family.
4. Search Swiss variants: two of Josef Anton's infants entered as *Spöni*, one burial as *Schöni*.
5. Push past Andreas Späni (d. 1696) — Rickenbach/Schwyz registers 1620s–1690s, Latin entries.

**Track 3 — the American generations**
1. **Sophia Fuhlendorf** is a second immigrant story: b. 24 Nov 1857 Schleswig-Holstein (Prussian annexation 1867 → heavy emigration). Find her arrival and parents; start with parental details on the 1882 St. Louis marriage record. Fuhlendorf is a rare, well-clustered north-German name.
2. Why Kansas? Sophia buried at **St. Mary's Cemetery, Chase** — a German-Catholic parish. Look for chain migration from Brooklyn/St. Louis into the same Rice Co. townships.
3. **Obituaries**: Charles (Apr 1939), Sophia (Jul 1914), Ernest (May 1964) in the *Lyons Daily News* and *Hutchinson News*. Small-town Kansas obituaries of that era routinely name the birth village and arrival year — cheapest route to Track 1's answer. Kansas Historical Society microfilm; Newspapers.com.
4. The Los Angeles detour: Charles in Cahuenga 1920, Rice Co. by 1930. **Barbara F. Spaeny (1908–1991)**, Inglewood Park Cemetery, LA — identify her.
5. **"Infant Spaeny"**, buried 22 Jul 1923, Alva Municipal Cemetery, Alva OK, plot 06-072-8 — three months after Ernest and Ora married there. Probably their first child, probably on no tree.
6. Interview the living: Michael in Hutchinson, and Mark's family in Springfield — Donald's war papers, the LA years, and whatever Ellis Island story was handed down. The cousin link is settled; the family memory behind it is not yet recorded.

## Added 7 Sep 2026 (second pass) — homeland, crest, graves

**Illgau founding families.** Gemeinde Illgau's official history ("Geschichte eines Bergdorfes") lists the eight family names that made up the community: Bürgler, Betschart, Heinzer, Rickenbacher, Horath, Martin, Schuler, **Späni**. Our line married Bürgler (1719, 1767) and Heinzer (1802). Illgau facts: Balmis 11th-c. settlement (oldest in canton); first named 1370 "Ilgoe"; Three Kings chapel early 1300s; parish recognised 1393 (Bishop Burkhard of Konstanz; first pastor Johannes Winsperg of Hall); separated from Muotathal 1676; registers baptisms 1697, marriages/burials 1698; 1788: 157 people in 22 houses; church consecrated 14 Oct 1893 (16 days before Ernest's birth); first road 1909–11; Sennenkilbi since 1903; arms = fire lily (Ilge) on green triple mount on red.

**Canton Schwyz context used:** 1291 Charter; 1315 Morgarten; name of Switzerland from Schwyz (official 1803); Kappel 1531 → hard Catholic; 1798 French invasion, Alois von Reding, capitulation 4/13 May; **1799 Suvorov fights through Muota valley directly below Illgau** — Johann Josef d. 25 Jun 1799, Johann Werner was 25; 1847 Sonderbund, Landsgemeinde abolished; 1848 Illgau political municipality; 19th c. Schwyz had 3rd-highest emigration rate in CH.

**Crest.** Martin Styger, *Wappenbuch des Kantons Schwyz* (Geneva 1936), records arms of all 569 Schwyz Geschlechter — Späni is one. Blazon NOT found online; do not invent. Staatsarchiv Schwyz sends B&W copy on request: **afk@sz.ch**. Schwyz arms typically evolved from Hausmarken (Archives Héraldiques Suisses 1991). J.P. Zwicky published an Ahnentafel for "the siblings Späni (von Innerthal)" in *Archiv für Schweizerische Familienkunde* — possible lead for another Späni line.

**"Named village"** = Heimatort/Bürgerort concept. Illgau (founding family) and Steinen (pre-1800 citizenship) on this line.

**Max Spaeny = Maximilian Späni** (b. 25 Aug 1867 Switzerland, d. 24 Jan 1949 Los Angeles; Inglewood Park; daughter Barbara F. 1908–1991, FG 72176843 / 72176879). Karl Anton's younger brother emigrated too → explains Charles in Cahuenga, LA on 1920 census.

### Corrected

1. **Illgau → Schwyz distance.** Page said "6 km by road". Wrong: the road descends to Schlattli
   in the Muota valley before turning for Schwyz. Straight-line is roughly 7 km; by road it is
   substantially more. Page now describes the route instead of asserting a figure.
2. **Canton Schwyz population.** Page said "~35,000 in 1833 → ~71,000 in 1950" — unsourced.
   Replaced with figures that actually bracket the emigration: **~34,000 in 1799 → ~55,000 in
   1900**.
3. **Emigration rate.** "Third-highest in Switzerland" ✓ confirmed — and it is across the
   **19th century**, behind **Ticino and Obwalden**, not specifically 1850–1900. Also found: a
   general crop failure in **1853** drove a mass wave to America, and in the **1880s** Schwyz
   flipped to net in-migration — meaning Karl Anton left at the tail of the wave, not its peak.
4. **Styger.** Page said he "published" the *Wappenbuch* in 1936. He died in **1935**; it was
   published posthumously, edited by his son **Paul Styger**. Martin Styger (1856–1935) of
   Rothenthurm was a lawyer, chancery director, and the canton's state archivist 1897–1900.
5. **"569 old Schwyz families."** Not stated by the Staatsarchiv, which says only *sämtliche
   Schwyzer-Geschlechter*. Figure removed; the claim that Späni is included is unaffected.
6. **Cable cars.** Page said 1952. Correct: **Ried–Illgau 1953** and **Illgau–Vorder Oberberg
   1955** (HLS). Also added: the **winter-safe road came only in 1973**, after which ~200
   villagers commuted down to Schwyz by 2000.
