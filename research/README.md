# Research files — the evidence base

**Everything on the website traces to a line in one of these files.** The pages are written
*from* the research, never the other way round. If a fact isn't here, it doesn't go on the site;
if it's here with a hedge, the hedge goes on the site too.

## The standard both lines are held to

**Three grades, used identically on every line.** They describe what is *attached* to a person,
not how likely the claim feels.

- **Record-backed** (`data-conf="doc"`) — indexed documents are attached: a census line, a parish
  entry, a marriage return, a headstone, a service record.
- **Partly sourced** (`data-conf="part"`) — something is attached, but thinly. One source, or
  sources covering only part of the claim.
- **Tree only** (`data-conf="tree"`) — a contributor entered them with no document behind it.
  Plausible, consistent, and not proof.

**Four rules on top of the grades.**

1. Nothing here has been checked against an original document image. Every citation is an index
   entry, and index entries carry transcription errors. Say so on the page.
2. "Probably" means probably. The hedge stays until a record removes it.
3. Living people stay thin — names and towns, nothing more.
4. A well-documented line lends no authority to a thin one. Grade each independently.

## How to work a new line

1. **Write down what the family already believes**, attributed — who said it, when. Family memory
   is a source; it is also the thing most often wrong in an interesting way. The paternal line's
   "Ellis Island" story was wrong about the place and right that something happened.
2. **Start from the most recent well-documented person and work backwards.** Never start from a
   surname match in someone else's tree.
3. **Obituaries first.** They are cheap, they name parents, siblings, towns and dates in one
   document, and small-town American obituaries of the early 20th century routinely name a birth
   village and year of arrival. This was the single highest-yield source on the paternal line.
4. **Then census, then vital records, then the homeland's parish or civil registers.**
5. **Record what you searched and found nothing in.** A negative result saves the next person a
   day, and it is the difference between "no record exists" and "nobody has looked".
6. **Log every correction with a date.** See the paternal file's "Verification pass" for format.

## Note on sourcing

Each file ends with a provenance section listing what it is built on, and a caveats section
stating what is weak. Keep both current. When a fact is corrected, don't quietly overwrite it —
record what it said before, what it says now, and what changed it.
